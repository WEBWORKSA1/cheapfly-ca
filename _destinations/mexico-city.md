Mexico City is one of the best-value long weekends or culture trips you can book from Canada. Nonstops leave Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver and Calgary, and once you land, food, transit and museums cost far less than in most North American cities. The cheapest fares usually turn up in the rainy late summer and in the quiet weeks after the holidays.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateways:** Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver on Air Canada and Aeroméxico, plus WestJet from Calgary.
- **Cheapest months:** late August to early October and mid-January to February.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $400–$550 return from Toronto, Montréal or Vancouver, taxes included.
- **Airport tip:** use an authorized prepaid taxi or a ride-hailing app from the official pick-up points.
- **Altitude:** the city sits about 2,240 m above sea level, so take your first day easy.

## Which Canadian airports fly to Mexico City

Mexico City International (MEX) is linked to [Toronto Pearson]({{ '/airports/yyz/' | relative_url }}), Montréal and [Vancouver]({{ '/airports/yvr/' | relative_url }}) by Air Canada and Aeroméxico, both of which have been adding capacity; Air Canada increased its Vancouver–Mexico City flights to 11 a week from June 2026. WestJet launched Calgary–Mexico City in May 2025, and Flair has also offered flights on some Canada–Mexico City routes.

From Ottawa, Halifax, Winnipeg and Edmonton, you'll usually connect in Toronto, Montréal, Calgary or a US hub. Frequencies change with the seasons (verify before you fly).

## Flight-only vs package

Mexico City is a flight-only destination for almost everyone; there are few beach-style packages. To see more of Mexico, consider an open-jaw: fly into Mexico City and home from [Cancún]({{ '/destinations/cancun/' | relative_url }}), Oaxaca or Puerto Vallarta, connecting with a cheap domestic flight or an overnight bus.

## Entry requirements in brief

Canadians don't need a visa for a typical tourist visit, but you need a valid passport. Mexico now records most air arrivals digitally instead of using a paper tourist card, and officers may ask about your return flight, accommodation and length of stay. Canada's travel advisory for Mexico includes regional warnings, so read it on travel.gc.ca before you go.

> **Heads up:** Use only authorized taxis or app-based rides, especially at night, and keep valuables out of sight on crowded transit.

## Money-saving tips for Mexico City

- **Eat the set lunch.** Many neighbourhood restaurants offer a multi-course fixed-price lunch at a very low price.
- **Ride the Metro and Metrobús.** Fares are among the cheapest of any big city; buy a rechargeable transit card at the airport or a station.
- **Use ride-hailing apps** for late nights or longer trips; they are usually cheaper and more transparent than street taxis.
- **Visit Teotihuacán by public bus** from the northern bus terminal instead of joining a pricey tour.
- **Use bank ATMs inside branches** and decline dynamic currency conversion. Our [currency converter]({{ '/tools/currency-converter/' | relative_url }}) helps you sanity-check prices in pesos.

## Getting from the airport

- **Authorized taxis:** buy a prepaid ticket at an official booth inside the terminal, then take the assigned cab. Prices depend on your destination zone.
- **Ride-hailing apps:** pick-ups operate from designated points at both terminals.
- **Metrobús Line 4:** serves both terminals and runs to the historic centre; you need a rechargeable card.
- **Metro Line 5:** the cheapest option, from a station near Terminal 1, but crowded and awkward with luggage, especially at rush hour.

Terminals 1 and 2 are separate, so check which one your airline uses before your return flight.

## Bottom line

Mexico City pairs short nonstop flights from four Canadian cities with exceptional value on the ground. Target late summer or January–February for the lowest fares, take an authorized taxi or app ride from the airport, and consider an open-jaw to see more of Mexico.
