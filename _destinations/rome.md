Rome is one of the most-requested European trips from Canada, and Toronto and Montréal have solid nonstop competition to get you there. Fares rise sharply for summer and Easter, so the cheapest tickets are usually found from November to March. Pair a winter or shoulder-season fare with smart open-jaw routing, and an Italy trip becomes much more affordable.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateways:** Toronto and Montréal, with nonstops on Air Canada, ITA Airways and seasonal Air Transat flights.
- **Cheapest months:** November to March, outside Christmas and Easter.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $650–$800 return from Toronto or Montréal, taxes included.
- **Smart routing:** fly into Rome and out of Milan or Venice to avoid backtracking.
- **Border:** visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180; ETIAS has not launched (verify before you fly).

## Which Canadian airports fly to Rome

[Toronto Pearson]({{ '/airports/yyz/' | relative_url }}) has the most Rome Fiumicino (FCO) nonstops: Air Canada flies year-round, ITA Airways joined the route in 2024, and Air Transat adds seasonal flights from spring to autumn. [Montréal]({{ '/airports/yul/' | relative_url }}) has seasonal service from Air Canada and Air Transat, whose program ran from April to October in 2026.

Seasonal summer nonstops from western Canada have come and gone in recent years, so travellers from Calgary, Edmonton and Vancouver usually connect via Toronto, Montréal or a European hub such as Frankfurt, Amsterdam or Paris. Air Canada also added Montréal–Catania in Sicily for summer 2026, a useful option if southern Italy is on your list. Confirm current schedules before you book (verify before you fly).

## Entry rules: EES and ETIAS

Italy is in the Schengen Area. The EU's Entry/Exit System has been fully operational since April 10, 2026, recording non-EU visitors' fingerprints and a facial photo on first entry, so allow extra time at passport control. ETIAS, the planned online authorisation for visa-exempt visitors such as Canadians, had no confirmed launch date as of November 2026.

> **Heads up:** Any website asking you to pay for ETIAS now is not official. Check travel.gc.ca before every trip.

## Flight-only vs package

Rome is mainly a flight-only destination, though Canadian tour operators sell Italy packages and escorted tours that bundle flights, hotels and transfers. These can be good value for first-time visitors in the shoulder season. Compare them against booking each piece separately.

## Money-saving tips for Rome

- **Drink coffee at the bar.** Standing at the counter costs less than table service.
- **Expect a cover charge.** Many restaurants add a per-person coperto; check the menu before you sit down.
- **Refill at public fountains.** Rome's drinking fountains provide free, cold water all over the city.
- **Look for free museum days.** State museums and sites have offered free entry on selected days, such as the first Sunday of the month, so check before you go.
- **Budget for the city tax.** Rome charges a per-person, per-night accommodation tax that varies by hotel category.
- **Book major sites ahead.** Timed tickets for the Vatican Museums and Colosseum avoid queues and resale markups.

## Getting from the airport

- **Leonardo Express:** non-stop trains to Termini station every 15 minutes or so, about 32 minutes, €14 one-way in 2026.
- **Regional trains:** cheaper trains (around €8) stop at Trastevere, Ostiense and Tiburtina but not Termini. Validate paper tickets before boarding.
- **Airport buses:** the cheapest option, but slower in traffic.
- **Taxis:** official white taxis charge a fixed fare from Fiumicino to the historic centre inside the Aurelian Walls (roughly €55 at last check). Confirm before you leave the rank.

## Bottom line

Rome is most affordable from Toronto and Montréal between November and March, when fares and crowds both drop. Use open-jaw routing to see more of Italy, take the train into the city, and allow time for the EU's new border checks.
