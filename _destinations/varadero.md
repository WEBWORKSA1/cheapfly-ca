Varadero has long been one of the cheapest all-inclusive beach holidays a Canadian could buy, with nonstops from across the country. That changed in 2026. As of November 2026, Canada's main airlines and tour operators have suspended Cuba flights until further notice because of severe jet fuel shortages, and the Government of Canada advises avoiding non-essential travel to Cuba.

## Quick answer

- **Current status:** Air Canada, WestJet, Sunwing and Air Transat suspended Cuba service in February 2026, and planned restart dates were later pushed back to "until further notice" (verify before you fly).
- **Advisory:** Canada advises avoiding non-essential travel to Cuba, citing fuel, power, food, water and medicine shortages.
- **New entrant:** the Canadian Transportation Agency approved Flair to operate Cuba flights, but no confirmed schedule was public at our last check.
- **Before 2026:** shoulder-season flights from Toronto or Montréal often sold for roughly $350–$500 return.
- **Alternatives:** Punta Cana, Cancún and Montego Bay offer similar beach weeks with plenty of nonstops.

## What happened to Varadero flights

In early February 2026, Cuban airports warned airlines that jet fuel would not be available. Air Canada suspended its Cuba flights first; WestJet, Sunwing and Air Transat followed, and the airlines ran empty aircraft south to bring Canadians home. Restart targets set for spring, then early summer, then autumn were all withdrawn, and the operators now describe the suspension as indefinite.

The underlying shortages also affect life on the ground: rolling blackouts, limited transport and patchy supplies at hotels. That is why the federal advisory was raised, and why insurers may limit cover for trips against an advisory.

> **Heads up:** If you held a Varadero booking that was cancelled, you are generally entitled to a refund or rebooking under the seller's policy and Canada's passenger protection rules. Our [APPR guide]({{ '/guides/canada-air-passenger-rights-appr-compensation/' | relative_url }}) explains your rights. This is general information, not professional advice.

## Which Canadian airports normally fly to Varadero

When service runs, Varadero's airport (VRA) is one of the most widely served sun destinations from Canada. Toronto, Montréal, Ottawa, Québec City, Halifax and Hamilton typically have the most winter flights, with seasonal nonstops from Winnipeg, Calgary and Edmonton. Most seats are sold inside packages by WestJet's vacation brands, Air Canada Vacations and Transat.

## Entry requirements to know

Under normal conditions, Canadians need a passport valid for the length of the stay, a Cuban tourist card (usually supplied by the tour operator or airline with a package), the online D'Viajeros traveller form completed before arrival, and proof of travel medical insurance. Cuba's entry procedures have been changing, so confirm the current rules on travel.gc.ca and with your operator before any future trip.

## Money and practical tips

- **Cash matters.** Card acceptance in Cuba is unreliable, and US-issued cards do not work. Travellers typically bring Canadian dollars or euros in cash.
- **Expect shortages.** Even in better times, resorts could run short of specific foods, toiletries and medicines, so pack what you need.
- **Check insurance wording.** Many policies treat trips against an "avoid non-essential travel" advisory differently. Read the exclusions before you buy.

## Getting from the airport

Varadero's airport sits roughly 25 km west of the resort strip, about a 30-minute drive. Packages include transfers. Taxis are available, but fuel shortages can make ground transport unpredictable. Havana is about two hours away by road.
