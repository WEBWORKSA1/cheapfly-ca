Orlando is the most popular US family destination for Canadians, and it is one of the shortest sun flights from eastern Canada. Softer Canadian demand for US travel in 2025–26 led airlines to trim some Florida flying, but it has also produced more seat sales on the routes that remain. The trick is timing your trip around school breaks and US holidays.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateways:** Toronto, Montréal, Ottawa and Hamilton, plus seasonal flights from Halifax and Québec City.
- **Cheapest months:** mid-January to mid-February, late April to May, and September to early November.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $250–$350 return from Toronto, Ottawa or Montréal, taxes included.
- **Entry:** passport or NEXUS card for air travel; US preclearance at major Canadian airports makes arrival quick.
- **Hidden costs:** park tickets, resort parking and road tolls often add more than the airfare.

## Which Canadian airports fly to Orlando

Orlando International (MCO) is served nonstop by Air Canada, WestJet, Porter and Air Transat from eastern Canada. Porter has been expanding its Florida flying, including more Hamilton–Orlando frequencies for winter 2026–27, which makes [Hamilton]({{ '/airports/yhm/' | relative_url }}) worth checking alongside Toronto Pearson. [Ottawa]({{ '/airports/yow/' | relative_url }}) also has year-round options.

The broader trend matters: industry data showed Canadian airlines cutting US capacity in 2026, with Flair reducing its US flying sharply and WestJet trimming its transborder schedule. Orlando lost some seats, so a route you used last year may not be running now (verify before you fly). Western Canadians usually connect via Toronto, Calgary or a US hub.

## Flight-only vs package

Orlando is mainly a flight-only market. Hotels and vacation rentals are plentiful, and park tickets are bought separately. Packages from Canadian tour operators can still be worth a look in quiet months, especially for families who want transfers handled. For keeping costs down with children, see our [flying with kids on a budget guide]({{ '/guides/flying-with-kids-on-a-budget/' | relative_url }}).

> **Pro tip:** Theme-park tickets use date-based pricing. The same weekday ticket can cost noticeably less in late January or early May than during March break, so pick travel dates with the park calendar open.

## Money-saving tips for Orlando

- **Stay off-site.** Hotels along International Drive and in Kissimmee often run free park shuttles and include breakfast.
- **Watch resort parking fees.** Many Orlando hotels charge daily parking, which can outweigh a cheaper room rate.
- **Handle tolls carefully.** Central Florida highways rely on electronic tolls, and rental-company toll programs add daily fees. Compare options before you pick up the car.
- **Use a no-FX-fee card** to avoid paying roughly 2.5% extra on every US purchase, and check rates with our [currency converter]({{ '/tools/currency-converter/' | relative_url }}). This is general information, not financial advice.
- **Buy groceries** for breakfasts and park snacks rather than paying park prices for everything.

## Entry requirements in brief

Canadians flying to the US need a valid passport or a NEXUS card. Since late December 2025, US officers photograph non-citizens, including Canadians, on entry and exit. Canadians staying 30 days or longer are subject to a US registration requirement; an air arrival record generally covers this, but snowbirds should confirm the details. US preclearance at major Canadian airports means you land in Orlando as a domestic arrival.

## Getting from the airport

- **Rideshare and taxis:** about 20 to 30 minutes to International Drive or the Disney area.
- **Shared shuttles:** pre-booked shuttle services run to the main resort areas.
- **LYNX buses:** the cheapest option, but slow with luggage.
- **Rental car:** best for beach days, Kennedy Space Center or Tampa trips.
- **Intercity rail:** trains leave from a station at the airport for West Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale and Miami, which makes it easy to fly into Orlando and home from South Florida.

## Bottom line

Orlando is a short, cheap hop from eastern Canada when you avoid March break, the holidays and peak summer. Check that your route still operates after 2026's US capacity cuts, price parking and tolls before you book, and line your dates up with the park calendar.
