Tokyo is more accessible from Canada than ever, with nonstops from Vancouver, Calgary, Toronto and Montréal and a low-cost option from the west coast. Fares are highest during cherry blossom season, Golden Week and summer, and lowest in late winter and June. Choose Haneda or Narita carefully, and you can save both money and travel time.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateway:** Vancouver, served by Air Canada, ANA, Japan Airlines and Zipair.
- **Also nonstop:** Calgary (WestJet, daily year-round), plus Toronto and Montréal (Air Canada).
- **Cheapest months:** mid-January to early March, June and early December.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $800–$1,000 return from Vancouver and $1,000–$1,200 from Toronto, taxes included.
- **New in 2026:** a higher departure tax (built into your ticket) and a refund-style tax-free shopping system from November 1.

## Which Canadian airports fly to Tokyo

Tokyo has two airports. Haneda (HND) is close to the city centre, while Narita (NRT) is about 60 km east. Both get Canadian nonstops.

[Vancouver]({{ '/airports/yvr/' | relative_url }}) is the strongest gateway: Air Canada flies to both Haneda and Narita, ANA flies daily to Haneda, Japan Airlines flies to Narita, and the low-cost Zipair flies to Narita several times a week. [Calgary]({{ '/airports/yyc/' | relative_url }}) has WestJet's daily, year-round Narita service, which connects with Japan Airlines flights onward through its codeshare. Air Canada also flies from Toronto to Tokyo and from Montréal to Narita.

Two upcoming changes are worth watching. Air Canada plans winter flights from Vancouver to Sapporo starting in December 2026, and Zipair has announced Toronto–Narita service for 2027. New competition usually brings launch fares (verify before you fly).

## Entry and 2026 changes

Canadians can visit Japan visa-free for short tourist stays, generally up to 90 days, with a valid passport. Completing the official online arrival registration before you land can speed up immigration and customs.

Japan's international departure tax rose from ¥1,000 to ¥3,000 on July 1, 2026; it's collected in your airfare. From November 1, 2026, tax-free shopping switched to a refund system: you pay the full price including the 10% consumption tax, then claim the refund at the airport before you leave.

> **Heads up:** Keep tax-free receipts organised by purchase and don't use or open tax-free consumables in Japan, or you may lose the refund. Check travel.gc.ca for current entry rules.

## Money-saving tips for Tokyo

- **Get an IC card.** Rechargeable transit cards (including visitor versions) work on trains, subways, buses and in convenience stores.
- **Rethink the national rail pass.** After big price increases in 2023, individual tickets or regional passes are often cheaper for a Tokyo-focused trip.
- **Eat set meals.** Lunch sets, ramen counters and convenience-store meals are excellent value.
- **Stay in business hotels.** Compact, clean rooms near major stations cost much less than international chains.
- **Enjoy free views.** The government building observation decks in Shinjuku are free.

## Getting from the airport

**Haneda:** the Keikyu Line reaches Shinagawa in about 15 to 20 minutes, and the Tokyo Monorail reaches Hamamatsucho. Both are cheap. Airport limousine buses serve major hotels.

**Narita:** the Keisei Skyliner reaches Ueno in about 40 minutes, the Narita Express serves Tokyo Station, Shinjuku and Shibuya, and slower Keisei trains and low-cost buses to Tokyo Station are the budget options.

## Bottom line

Tokyo is most affordable from Vancouver and Calgary, and in late winter, June and early December.
