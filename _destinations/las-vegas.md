Las Vegas is one of the cheapest sunny city breaks from western Canada, with short nonstops from Vancouver, Calgary and Edmonton and frequent seat sales. Canadian demand for US travel softened in 2025–26, and airlines responded by cutting some routes, so the map has changed. The good news: flexible travellers can still find low fares, especially midweek and outside big event weeks.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateways:** Vancouver, Calgary and Edmonton, with nonstops from Toronto, Montréal and Winnipeg on some carriers.
- **What changed:** WestJet dropped several Las Vegas routes, including Toronto, Regina and Saskatoon, from its winter 2026–27 schedule; Porter is launching Edmonton–Las Vegas from November 16, 2026.
- **Cheapest timing:** midweek, and especially summer and quiet weeks in December and January.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $250–$350 return from Alberta or Vancouver, taxes included.
- **Biggest hidden cost:** mandatory hotel resort fees, not the airfare.

## Which Canadian airports fly to Las Vegas

The main airport (LAS) gets Canadian service from Air Canada, WestJet, Flair and Porter, plus US carriers via their hubs. Western Canada has the strongest schedule, led by [Vancouver]({{ '/airports/yvr/' | relative_url }}) and the two Alberta airports.

Porter's new four-times-weekly route from [Edmonton]({{ '/airports/yeg/' | relative_url }}), starting November 16, 2026, is its first international flying from Alberta and adds competition just as other carriers pulled back. Industry data showed Las Vegas among the US leisure markets that lost the most Canadian seats in 2026, and Flair cut its US flying by more than half. Always confirm a route is operating before you plan around it (verify before you fly). From Atlantic Canada, expect one stop.

## Flight-only vs package

Las Vegas packages from Canadian airlines and travel sites can be good value, because operators negotiate room rates with large hotels. Always compare a package with the flight plus the hotel's total nightly price, including resort fees and taxes. Packages tend to win on weekends and during busy periods, while flight-only plus a midweek hotel deal often wins in summer.

## Money-saving tips for Las Vegas

- **Compare total room prices.** Mandatory daily resort fees can add a large amount per night. Some properties off the Strip or downtown skip them or charge less.
- **Stay midweek.** Sunday-to-Thursday nights are often a fraction of Friday and Saturday rates.
- **Ride transit.** Public buses run along the Strip and to downtown around the clock, with multi-day passes, and some resort groups run free trams between properties.
- **Eat off the Strip.** The Chinatown area west of the Strip has excellent, cheaper restaurants.
- **Use discount show booths.** Same-day ticket booths sell reduced-price seats for many shows.
- **Plan park fees.** US national parks announced higher entry fees for non-US residents at some of the busiest parks, such as Zion and Grand Canyon, starting in 2026; check the park service website before you go.

## Entry requirements in brief

Canadians flying to the US need a valid passport or a NEXUS card. Since late December 2025, US officers photograph non-citizens, including Canadians, on entry and exit. Stays of 30 days or longer carry a US registration requirement. US preclearance at major Canadian airports means you arrive in Las Vegas as a domestic passenger.

## Getting from the airport

- **Public bus:** regional transit buses connect the airport with the Strip and downtown for a few US dollars.
- **Rideshare:** pick-up zones are in the parking garages; usually the simplest door-to-door option.
- **Taxi:** short ride to the Strip; ask for surface streets rather than the freeway to keep the fare down.
- **Shared shuttles:** budget-friendly for solo travellers.
- **Rental car:** only worthwhile if you plan desert day trips.

> **Heads up:** Las Vegas summer heat regularly passes 40°C. Plan hikes and outdoor trips for early morning, and carry plenty of water.
