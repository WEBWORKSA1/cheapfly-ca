Lisbon has become one of the best-value European trips from Canada. Toronto and Montréal have strong nonstop competition from TAP Air Portugal, Air Canada and Air Transat, and Halifax gained a summer link in 2026. Combine that with mild winters, reasonable prices on the ground and a handy stopover option, and Lisbon often costs less than Paris, London or Rome.

## Quick answer

- **Best gateways:** Toronto and Montréal year-round, plus seasonal summer service from Halifax.
- **Cheapest months:** November to March, outside Christmas and Easter.
- **Deal threshold:** roughly $550–$700 return from Toronto or Montréal, taxes included.
- **Bonus:** TAP's stopover program can add Lisbon to a trip elsewhere in Europe.
- **Border:** visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180; ETIAS has not launched (verify before you fly).

## Which Canadian airports fly to Lisbon

[Toronto Pearson]({{ '/airports/yyz/' | relative_url }}) and [Montréal]({{ '/airports/yul/' | relative_url }}) each have nonstop Lisbon (LIS) flights on TAP Air Portugal, Air Canada and Air Transat, and Air Canada announced extra Montréal–Lisbon frequencies for winter 2026–27. Three airlines on the same routes is good news for fares.

WestJet added Halifax–Lisbon to its summer 2026 schedule, part of a larger push to use Halifax as an Atlantic gateway to Europe. Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver and Winnipeg travellers usually connect through Toronto, Montréal or a European hub. Seasonal routes change each year, so confirm current schedules (verify before you fly).

## Entry rules: EES and ETIAS

Portugal is in the Schengen Area. The EU's Entry/Exit System has been fully operational since April 10, 2026, recording non-EU visitors' fingerprints and a facial photo on first entry instead of stamping passports. ETIAS, the planned online authorisation for visa-exempt travellers such as Canadians, had no confirmed launch date as of November 2026, and a transition period is planned once it starts.

> **Heads up:** Ignore any site asking you to pay for ETIAS now. Check travel.gc.ca before every trip.

## Flight-only vs package

Lisbon is mainly a flight-only trip. Some Canadian tour operators sell Portugal packages that combine flights with hotels in Lisbon, Porto or the Algarve, and they can be good value in the off-season. Compare them against booking separately.

## Money-saving tips for Lisbon

- **Get a reloadable transit card.** Lisbon's rechargeable card works on the metro, buses, trams and many ferries, and day passes can pay off if you ride often.
- **Ride regular routes, not the famous tram queue.** Scenic tram lines get crowded; standard buses and trams cover much of the same ground.
- **Watch the couvert.** Bread, olives and cheese placed on your table are usually charged if you eat them; send them back if you don't want them.
- **Eat the daily special.** Many neighbourhood restaurants offer an affordable lunch special.
- **Take the train to Sintra or Cascais.** Suburban trains cost a fraction of a day tour.
- **Budget for tourist tax.** Lisbon charges a municipal tourist tax per person per night, capped at a set number of nights.

## Getting from the airport

- **Metro:** the red line runs from the airport station into the city in about 20 to 30 minutes, with connections to the other lines. It's the cheapest option.
- **City buses:** several routes serve the airport, though space for luggage is limited.
- **Taxis and rideshare:** the airport is only about 7 km from the centre, so fares are moderate; metered taxis add a night tariff.
- **Dedicated airport shuttle:** guides report the former airport express bus no longer runs, so plan on the metro or a taxi.

## Bottom line

Lisbon offers some of the best transatlantic value from Toronto and Montréal, especially from November to March. Watch for fares near the deal thresholds above, consider a stopover or open-jaw, and ride the metro from the airport.
