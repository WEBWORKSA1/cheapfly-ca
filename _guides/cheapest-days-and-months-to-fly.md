The cheapest days to fly from Canada are usually Tuesday, Wednesday and often Saturday, and the cheapest months tend to be late January to February and late October to mid-December, outside of holidays. The exact pattern depends on your destination, so it pays to know the high and low seasons for the region you're visiting.

This guide covers day-of-week patterns, a region-by-region month table, the Canadian holiday traps that catch travellers every year, and the shoulder seasons that offer the best value.

## Quick answer

- **Cheapest days:** Tuesday and Wednesday, plus Saturday on many leisure routes.
- **Priciest days:** Friday and Sunday, especially around long weekends.
- **Cheapest months overall:** late January to February and late October to mid-December, excluding school breaks and holidays.
- **Biggest traps:** Christmas to New Year, March Break, Easter and long weekends.
- **Best value:** shoulder seasons such as late April to early June and September to October.

## The cheapest days of the week to fly

Airlines price seats based on demand, and demand follows the work week. Business travellers fill flights on Monday mornings and Thursday or Friday evenings, while leisure travellers crowd Friday departures and Sunday returns. Midweek flights fill more slowly, so they're more likely to have cheaper fare buckets open.

| Day | Typical demand | What it means for price |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | High on business routes | Morning flights can be pricey |
| Tuesday | Lower | Often among the cheapest |
| Wednesday | Lower | Often among the cheapest |
| Thursday | Rising | Evening flights fill with weekenders |
| Friday | High | Usually one of the most expensive days |
| Saturday | Lower on many routes | Good value for leisure trips |
| Sunday | High | Popular return day, often expensive |

Time of day matters too. Very early departures and overnight flights are less popular, so they sometimes price lower. On long weekends, the pattern shifts: the days just before and after the holiday become the pricey ones.

> **Pro tip:** Search a fare calendar with your trip length set, then slide the dates a day or two in each direction. Moving a Sunday return to Monday or Tuesday is one of the easiest savings available.

## Does the day you book matter?

Much less than the day you fly. You'll still hear that booking on a Tuesday is cheaper, and some airline sales do launch early in the week, but fares move every day as seats sell. Booking when you see a good price beats waiting for a particular weekday. For sun destinations, the cheapest months overlap with hurricane season, which runs from June through November and is most active from about August to October. That doesn't mean you shouldn't go, but it does mean you should buy good travel insurance and keep plans flexible. ## Holiday traps for Canadian travellers

The most expensive dates are predictable. Mark these on your calendar before you start searching.

### Christmas and New Year

Roughly the week before Christmas through the first weekend of January is peak season on nearly every route. Flying on Christmas Day or New Year's Day itself can be cheaper, if your plans allow it.

### March Break

Spring break isn't one week across Canada. Ontario's is usually mid-March, Québec's often falls in late February or early March, British Columbia's typically spans two weeks in March, and other provinces and school boards set their own dates. The result is several weeks of high demand for sun and Florida routes, so check your local school calendar and avoid those dates if you can.

### Easter and spring long weekends

Easter weekend pushes up domestic and sun fares, as does Victoria Day in late May.

### Summer long weekends

Canada Day, the August civic holiday in most provinces and Labour Day all create short demand spikes on domestic routes, layered on top of already-high summer prices.

### Thanksgiving, twice

Canadian Thanksgiving on the second Monday of October affects domestic routes. US Thanksgiving, on the fourth Thursday of November, drives up prices on flights to American cities.

### Holidays at your destination

Holidays abroad matter too. Lunar New Year pushes up fares across much of East Asia, Golden Week fills Japan in late April and early May, and parts of Europe get very busy in August when many locals take vacation.

> **Heads up:** Big events such as major conferences, festivals and sports tournaments can make a normally cheap weekend expensive. If a fare looks oddly high for an off-peak date, check what's happening at your destination.

## Shoulder seasons: the sweet spot

Shoulder seasons sit between peak and low season. Weather is usually decent, crowds are thinner, and airlines are more likely to run sales.

- **Europe:** April, May, September and October offer mild weather and much lower fares than summer.
- **Sun destinations:** late April to May, and November before the holidays, combine good weather with reasonable prices.
- **Domestic Canada:** late spring and September are great times to explore the Rockies, the East Coast or Québec without summer pricing.
- **Japan:** late May and early fall can be good value, while cherry blossom and autumn leaf seasons draw crowds. Our [Tokyo destination guide]({{ '/destinations/tokyo/' | relative_url }}) has more detail.

> **Example:** A family heading from Calgary to Mexico for a week in mid-March might find fares well above what the same trip costs in early May. If school schedules allow, shifting the trip to the shoulder season can free up money for a nicer hotel.

## Putting it together

1. Identify the peak periods for your destination and your province's school calendar.
2. Aim for the shoulder season if weather matters, or the low season if price matters most.
3. Search a fare calendar and favour Tuesday, Wednesday or Saturday departures.
4. Compare against what a good fare looks like for your route.
5. Set alerts so you don't miss a sale; [CheapFly's free deal alerts]({{ '/deal-alerts/' | relative_url }}) flag off-peak bargains from Canadian airports.

## Bottom line

Flying midweek and outside holiday periods is the simplest way to save on almost any route from Canada. Learn the peak dates for your destination, steer toward shoulder seasons when you can, and stay flexible by a day or two. Those small shifts add up to real savings over a year of travel.
