Canadians get the cheapest sun vacations by booking flight-plus-hotel packages in the shoulder and low seasons, and by being flexible enough to pounce on last-minute sell-offs. September through mid-December, and May through June, usually deliver the best value, while school holidays carry the highest prices. This guide explains how the package market works and how to tell a real deal from a dressed-up regular price.

## Key takeaways

- **Packages usually beat DIY** for mainstream all-inclusive resorts, thanks to bulk contracts on seats and rooms.
- **The cheapest months** are September and October, with good value in May, June and late fall.
- **Last-minute sell-offs** are real outside peak weeks, if you are flexible.
- **Hurricane season** brings low prices and real risk; insurance and a flexible mindset are essential.
- **Read the inclusions** carefully, because taxes, bags and premium extras can add up.

## Who sells sun packages in Canada in 2026

The Canadian package market has consolidated in recent years. The main players as of late 2026:

- **Air Canada Vacations**, using Air Canada and Air Canada Rouge flights.
- **WestJet Vacations** and **WestJet Vacations Quebec**, part of the WestJet Group.
- **Sunwing Vacations**, also part of the WestJet Group. Sunwing Airlines was integrated into WestJet, so Sunwing packages now fly on WestJet-operated aircraft, and the former Signature Vacations brand was folded into Sunwing.
- **Transat**, using Air Transat flights; Transat has also announced packages on Porter flights from Toronto, Ottawa and Hamilton starting in winter 2026–27.
- **Travel agencies, online travel agencies and sell-off specialists**, which resell these operators' inventory and sometimes add their own promotions.

Because several retailers often sell the same tour operator's package, compare the same resort and dates across a few of them; prices and perks can differ.

## Package vs flight plus hotel

| Factor | Package | Flight + hotel separately |
|---|---|---|
| Price for big all-inclusive resorts | Often lower | Often higher |
| Airport transfers | Usually included | Extra cost |
| Flexibility on flights | Limited to the operator's schedule | Any airline, any day |
| Boutique or small hotels | Limited choice | Wide choice |
| Using points | Rarely | Easy for flights and some hotels |
| Help during disruptions | Tour operator reps on site | You handle it yourself |

Packages win most often for a standard one- or two-week all-inclusive stay. Building your own trip makes more sense for city stays, longer trips, unusual hotels, or when you can cover the flight with points.

## Last-minute sell-offs: how they work

Tour operators commit to aircraft seats and hotel room blocks months ahead. When a departure is not full in the final weeks, discounting the leftovers is better than flying empty seats. That is where last-minute deals come from.

To catch them:

- Be flexible on resort, star rating and even destination country.
- Search from the largest airport you can reasonably reach.
- Watch the one-to-three-week window before departure.
- Avoid school holidays, when operators rarely need to discount.

## Early booking or last minute: which should you choose?

Both strategies work, but for different travellers.

**Book early (four to ten months ahead) if** you must travel during school holidays, want a specific resort or room type, are booking for a family or group, or need a particular departure airport. Operators often run early-booking promotions with perks such as reduced deposits, free upgrades or price-drop protection, and the most popular resorts sell out first.

**Wait for the last minute if** you can travel outside peak weeks, are flexible on resort and destination, and live near a large airport with many weekly departures. The savings can be significant, but the choice shrinks as departure approaches.

A middle path works for many couples: pick two or three acceptable resorts, track their prices for a few weeks, and book when one drops below the level you have seen before.

## Your departure airport matters

Package prices can differ noticeably between Canadian cities for the same resort and week. Big gateways such as Toronto, Montréal, Calgary and Vancouver have the most flights and therefore the most discounting. Smaller cities may have only one or two weekly departures in winter, which limits sell-offs. If you live within driving distance of two airports, always price both, and remember to include parking or transit costs in the comparison.

## Hurricane season: the risk and how to manage it

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from **June 1 to November 30**, with the most activity usually between mid-August and October. Pacific-coast resorts in Mexico, such as Los Cabos and Puerto Vallarta, face their own eastern Pacific storm season over a similar period.

Most trips in these months go ahead without trouble, which is why prices are attractive. To protect yourself:

- Buy travel insurance at the time of booking, including trip cancellation and interruption.
- Understand that coverage usually stops for a storm once it is named, so buy early.
- Check your tour operator's hurricane policy for rebooking or credits.
- Follow travel advisories on travel.gc.ca.

> **Heads up:** In 2026, Canadian airlines suspended Cuba flights for months because of aviation fuel shortages on the island, then announced phased returns for the winter season. If you are considering a Cuba package, confirm the current status with the operator and on travel.gc.ca.

## The fees that hide in "all-inclusive"

An all-inclusive package covers flights, hotel, meals, drinks and usually transfers, but several costs can still appear:

- **Local visitor or tourist taxes** collected separately in some destinations.
- **Checked bags and seat selection** on basic package fares, which some operators now unbundle.
- **À la carte or premium restaurants**, top-shelf drinks and room service at some resorts.
- **Excursions, spa services and resort Wi-Fi** at some properties.
- **Tips** for staff, which many Canadians budget for.

Add these up for every package you are considering before you compare headline prices.

## How to spot a real deal

- **Check the same resort on several sites.** "Was" prices are often inflated.
- **Read recent reviews**, focusing on the last few months.
- **Confirm the room category** and whether the price is per person based on double occupancy.
- **Look at transfer time**; a cheap resort two hours from the airport costs you vacation time.
- **Ask about price-drop policies.** Some operators offer credits if the price falls after you book.

> **Example:** Two packages to the same Dominican Republic resort cost almost the same, but one includes a checked bag and seat selection while the other does not. For a couple, those extras can easily outweigh a small headline price difference.
