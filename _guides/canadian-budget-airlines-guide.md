Canada's budget airline scene has shrunk and reshuffled in recent years, but low fares haven't disappeared. In 2026, Flair Airlines is the main independent low-fare carrier, Porter competes hard on price, and the big airlines sell bare-bones basic fares that can rival anything the budget carriers offer.

The catch is that the lowest fare on screen rarely includes everything you need. This guide explains who's flying, how fare families work, which add-on fees matter most and how to compare the true cost of a trip.

## Quick answer

- **Independent low-fare carrier:** Flair Airlines, mostly domestic, US sun and Mexico and Caribbean routes.
- **Price-competitive hybrid:** Porter Airlines, now flying from Toronto Pearson and across western Canada as well as its Billy Bishop base.
- **Basic fares from the big players:** Air Canada (including its leisure arm, Air Canada Rouge), WestJet and Air Transat.
- **Gone:** Swoop (merged into WestJet in 2023), Lynx Air (ceased in early 2024) and Canada Jetlines (ceased in 2024). Sunwing's flying has been folded into WestJet.
- **Golden rule:** compare the total price for the way you'll actually travel, not the base fare.

## Who's flying in 2026

### Flair Airlines

Based in Edmonton and flying a fleet of roughly 20 Boeing 737s, Flair is the closest thing Canada has to a classic low-cost carrier. Its leaders have started describing it as a "value carrier" rather than ultra-low-cost, but the model is still unbundled: a low base fare with extras sold separately. It flies between major Canadian cities and to sun and US leisure destinations.

### Porter Airlines

Porter built its name at Toronto's downtown Billy Bishop airport and has since expanded with larger jets to Toronto Pearson, western Canada, the US and sun destinations. It positions itself as a more comfortable option, with complimentary snacks and drinks and no middle seats, but its cheapest fare still leaves out a carry-on.

### WestJet

WestJet absorbed its former low-cost arm, Swoop, in 2023 and has since been integrating Sunwing's flying. Its lowest fare, UltraBasic, is aimed squarely at budget-conscious travellers.

### Air Canada and Air Canada Rouge

Air Canada sells an Economy Basic fare on most routes. Rouge, its leisure airline, flies many sun and vacation routes and is moving to an all-Boeing 737 MAX fleet. Fare families are the same whichever brand operates the flight.

### Air Transat

Montréal-based Air Transat focuses on sun and European leisure routes. Its entry-level Eco Budget fare is the cheapest way onto its flights, with tighter baggage rules than its higher fares.

## How the cheapest fares compare

Baggage rules on basic fares change often, so treat this as a snapshot and verify before you fly.

| Airline | Cheapest fare | Carry-on on cheapest fare? | Points of note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flair Airlines | Base fare | Personal item only; carry-on extra | Most extras sold individually |
| Porter Airlines | PorterClassic Basic | Personal item only; carry-on extra | No middle seats, snacks included |
| WestJet | UltraBasic | Personal item only on most routes; carry-on allowed to Europe and Asia | Carry-ons taken to the gate face a bag fee plus a surcharge |
| Air Canada and Rouge | Economy Basic | Personal item only on North American and sun routes (tickets bought since January 2025) | Some status holders and cardholders exempt |
| Air Transat | Eco Budget | Personal item only; other bags cost extra | Leisure-focused network |

The common thread is clear: the cheapest ticket on most Canadian airlines now assumes you'll travel with just a small bag under the seat.

## How fare families work

Most Canadian airlines sell several fare levels on the same flight. As you move up, you typically gain:

- A full-size carry-on bag
- A checked bag (on mid-tier or flexible fares)
- Free or cheaper seat selection
- The ability to change or cancel, sometimes for a fee, sometimes free
- More loyalty points and better boarding groups

The lowest tier usually boards last, can't be changed or refunded, and may earn few or no points. The gap between basic and the next fare up varies widely by route and date, which is why comparing matters.

> **Heads up:** Some basic fares can't be changed at all, even for a fee. If there's any chance your plans will shift, the cheapest fare may be the most expensive one you can buy.

## The add-on fees that change the math

These are the fees that most often turn a cheap fare into an average one:

- **Carry-on bags.** Usually cheaper bought online at booking than later, and far more expensive at the gate.
- **Checked bags.** Prices often rise as departure approaches and are highest at the airport.
- **Seat selection.** Optional on most airlines, but families who want to sit together may need to pay.
- **Changes and cancellations.** Often not allowed on basic fares, or expensive enough to wipe out your savings.
- **Onboard food and drink.** Not a big cost, but worth a thought on a long sun flight.

Before you fly, check your bag with our [carry-on size checker]({{ '/tools/carry-on-checker/' | relative_url }}). Personal-item limits are strict, and a bag that's slightly too big can cost you more at the gate than the fare itself.

## How to compare the true cost

Price the trip exactly the way you'll travel, side by side. Here's a hypothetical comparison for one traveller flying a round trip with a carry-on and a preferred seat.

| Cost item | Airline A, basic fare | Airline B, standard fare |
|---|---|---|
| Base round-trip fare with taxes | $258 | $318 |
| Carry-on bag (both directions) | $90 | Included |
| Seat selection (both directions) | $40 | $40 |
| Total | $388 | $358 |

Airline A looked $60 cheaper in search results but ends up $30 more expensive. If you only need a backpack and don't care where you sit, Airline A wins. The answer depends entirely on how you travel.

> **Pro tip:** Before you search, write down exactly what you'll bring and whether you need to choose seats. Then use that checklist on every airline's booking page, so you're comparing the same trip each time.

## When budget makes sense

Low fares are a great fit when:

- You can travel with only a personal item or a single carry-on.
- Your dates are firm and you're unlikely to change them.
- You're flying a short route where comfort extras don't matter much.
- You have backup options if a flight is cancelled, such as a flexible return date.

They're a poorer fit when you're checking multiple bags, travelling with young children who need seats together, connecting to a non-refundable cruise or tour, or flying on a route the airline serves only a few times a week, since a cancellation can mean waiting days for the next flight.

## Budget airlines and your passenger rights

Every airline flying to, from or within Canada must follow the Air Passenger Protection Regulations, regardless of fare type. Smaller airlines owe lower compensation for delays and cancellations than large ones, but denied boarding compensation is the same for everyone. ## Tips for flying budget without regret

1. Pack to the personal-item limit if your fare doesn't include a carry-on; our [carry-on-only packing guide]({{ '/guides/carry-on-only-packing-guide/' | relative_url }}) shows how.
2. Buy any bags online at booking, not at the airport.
3. Check in online and download your boarding pass to avoid counter fees where they apply.
4. Pack snacks and an empty water bottle to fill after security.
5. Watch for seat sales, which often hit low-fare carriers first. [Join CheapFly's free deal alerts]({{ '/deal-alerts/' | relative_url }}) to hear about them.

> **Example:** Two friends flying Vancouver to Calgary for a weekend with backpacks only could save meaningfully on basic fares. A family of four heading to Cancún with checked bags may find a standard fare or a package works out about the same once every extra is added.

## Bottom line

Canada's budget airline landscape is smaller than it was a few years ago, but low fares are still out there from Flair, Porter and the basic fares of the bigger airlines. The trick is to price the whole trip, not just the seat. Travel light and stay flexible, and budget fares can deliver genuine savings.
