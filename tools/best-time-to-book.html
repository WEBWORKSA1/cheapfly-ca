How the booking window works

Airlines release seats roughly a year ahead and adjust prices as seats sell. Far from departure, fares tend to sit at a steady middle price. As the date approaches, airlines run sales to fill weak flights — that is the sweet spot. In the final weeks, remaining seats are priced for travellers who must fly, and fares climb quickly.

The window shifts with distance and demand. Short domestic and transborder trips have a short, late window. Long-haul trips to Europe and Asia reward earlier planning, especially in summer. Peak periods — Christmas, March Break and July–August — move every window earlier because seats sell out regardless of price.
