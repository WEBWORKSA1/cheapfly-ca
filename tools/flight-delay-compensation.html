How Canadian flight compensation works

Canada's Air Passenger Protection Regulations apply to flights to, from and within Canada on any airline. What you are owed depends on two things: the category of the disruption and how late you arrive at your final destination — not how late you leave.

  • Within the airline's control (crew shortages, overbooking, routine maintenance): compensation, care while you wait and rebooking or refund.
  • Within control but required for safety (a mechanical problem found outside routine checks): no compensation, but care and rebooking or refund.
  • Outside the airline's control (weather, air traffic control, security): no compensation and no meals or hotel, but the airline must rebook you, or refund you if it cannot rebook you within 48 hours.

How to claim, step by step

  1. Collect your boarding passes, booking confirmation, the airline's delay messages and receipts for meals or hotels.
  2. Submit a written claim on the airline's compensation form within one year. State the flight, date, arrival delay and the amount you believe you are owed.
  3. Wait up to 30 days for the airline's answer. It must pay or explain why it owes nothing.
  4. If you disagree, file a complaint with the Canadian Transportation Agency. Backlogs are long, so be patient and keep copies of everything.
