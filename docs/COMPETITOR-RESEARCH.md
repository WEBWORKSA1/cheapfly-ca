# CheapFly.ca competitor and market research

Compiled: 2026-11-01. Scope: flight-deal newsletters, metasearch engines and online travel agencies (OTAs), points and travel-hacking publishers, passenger-rights resources, and the content sites that now rank for CheapFly's target topics.

## Read this first: method and limitations

The brief asked for at least 35 sites to be opened directly (WebFetch). **That was not possible in this research environment:**

- **0 sites could be fetched.** WebFetch failed DNS resolution for every external host tried: going.com, jacksflightclub.com, thriftytraveler.com, dollarflightclub.com, secretflying.com, theflightdeal.com, nextdeparture.ca and yyzdeals.com. The agent proxy also denied `www.youtube.com`. These hosts would have to be allowed in the session's network settings.
- **Web search worked, but the shared budget ran out.** The limit of 200 searches per turn is shared by all agents, and it was used up while APPR, airline-status and baggage facts were being checked. Only one search was spent directly on a competitor (Going).

So that the document is still useful, every profile below is tagged by evidence type:

- **[S] Seen this session:** taken from search-engine results returned on 2026-11-01, meaning the page titles, URLs and the search tool's extracts of those pages. About 25 of the 41 profiles have at least one [S] observation. Many come from the searches run for APPR, baggage and airline facts, which also show **who ranks for CheapFly's target topics in 2026**.
- **[K] Background, not re-verified:** general knowledge of the site as of mid-2026. These items describe long-standing, well-known features and avoid exact prices or counts. **Re-check every [K] item** in a browser before quoting it publicly or basing a big decision on it.

No profile below repeats a competitor's marketing claim as fact. Where a competitor's own claim is mentioned, it is labelled as theirs.

## Part 1: Site profiles (41)

### A. Flight-deal newsletters and deal sites

#### 1. Going (formerly Scott's Cheap Flights)
- **URL:** https://www.going.com · **Category:** Paid and free flight-deal alert membership (US-centric, accepts Canadian airports)
- **Features:**
  - [S] Three tiers: **Limited (free)**, **Premium** and **Elite**. Premium is listed at **US$49/year**. Elite is quoted at about US$199/year by one guide, which could not be confirmed on an official page.
  - [S] **14-day free trial** of Premium or Elite, one per account. It **auto-converts to a paid annual plan on day 15**, so users must cancel in account settings before then.
  - [S] Premium adds a personalized **Watchlist** (deals for chosen destinations) and **"Going with Points"** award-flight alerts.
  - [S] Marketing uses **savings claims** (the company says Premium members save about $550 per international ticket) and a crowd-proof headline on a landing page ("2M+ travelers already book this way").
  - [S] Separate help centre (help.going.com) with articles such as "What are the membership tiers?" and "Who is Going". There is a public Trustpilot presence with several hundred reviews.
  - [S] Promo codes are spread through coupon and deal sites (Groupon, coupons.com, DealNews), which is a sign of affiliate and partner distribution.
  - [K] Onboarding asks for the home airport(s) first, then the email. Deal emails explain why the fare is good, give sample dates and say how to book (the user books directly with the airline or OTA).
- **Monetization:** Subscriptions (free-to-paid upsell), plus partner and affiliate promotions.
- **Adopt:** A free tier keyed to **home airport selection**, plus a plain-language "what makes a deal" explainer on every alert. Never use auto-renewing trials without a clear reminder: Going's trial-conversion mechanics are effective but draw complaints.

- **Features:**

- **Monetization:** Subscriptions.
- **Adopt:** Put **"How to book this"** steps and example dates in every deal alert, so readers can act in under 2 minutes.

### B. Metasearch engines and OTAs

#### 14. Google Flights

### D. Passenger-rights resources

#### 30. Canadian Transportation Agency / rppa-appr.ca
- **URL:** https://rppa-appr.ca and https://otc-cta.gc.ca · **Category:** Official regulator information
- **Features:**
  - [S] Plain-language pages for **compensation for delays and cancellations**, and the regulation summary.
  - [S] States carrier size is in the airline's tariff, and that claims must be made in writing within 1 year.
  - [S] Publishes a forward regulatory plan, a "What We Heard" consultation report and a 2026 chair's transition book that discusses the pending APPR overhaul.
  - [K] Online complaint form, used after the airline claim fails.
- **Monetization:** None (government).
- **Adopt:** Link to it as the authoritative source. Add a **"Status of pending APPR changes"** box on the passenger-rights guide and tool (see FACT-CHECK.md), since most commercial pages blur proposed rules with current ones.

#### 31. AirHelp
- **URL:** https://www.airhelp.com · **Category:** Flight-compensation claims company
- **Features:**

  - [K] **Multi-step eligibility checker**: flight, then disruption, then details, then email capture.
  - [K] "No win, no fee" commission model, with a fee percentage taken from successful claims.
  - [K] Partner or affiliate program for publishers.
- **Monetization:** Commission on successful claims.
- **Adopt:** Make /tools/flight-delay-compensation/ a **multi-step checker that ends with a free result**: "you may be owed $X; here's how to claim it yourself for free". The deal-alert opt-in is offered after the result, not before.

#### 32. Flightright

- **Adopt:** Add a **"data discrepancy" note** where airline pages disagree (we found this for WestJet, PAL and Air Transat). Readers trust a site that admits the mess and resolves it.

### (b) Lead-gen and conversion patterns that work

**Copy angles that fit CheapFly's rules (no fabricated numbers):**
- **Specific and local:** "Cheap-flight alerts from YOUR airport: Toronto, Hamilton, even Buffalo." Specific airports beat generic "cheap flights".
- **Effort-saving:** "We watch fares so you don't have to refresh Google Flights every night."
- **Explain the threshold:** "We only send a deal when the fare drops well below its usual range for that route." State the method, not a savings claim.
- **Risk reversal:** "Free. One email when there's something worth booking. Unsubscribe in one click."
- **Avoid** "members save $550" style claims unless CheapFly can measure them honestly. The content spec forbids fabricated savings numbers.

**Form mechanics:**
- **Two-step form:** step 1 is "Where do you fly from?" (airport chips: YYZ, YUL, YVR and so on, multi-select, plus "nearby US airports" as a toggle). Step 2 is email only. Starting with a low-friction, fun choice raises completion. Going and the Canadian city-deal sites lead with airport choice.
- **Optional step 3 after confirmation:** interests (sun, Europe, Asia, USA city breaks, points and award deals) and travel months. Asking for this after signup keeps the first conversion fast.
- **Placement:** inline after the "Quick answer" section, at the end of the article (after "Bottom line"), and a non-intrusive sticky bar on mobile. Avoid entry pop-ups. They hurt mobile UX and Google's intrusive-interstitial guidance applies.
- **Post-result capture on tools:** show the full answer first (compensation estimate, carry-on fit), then offer "Get alerts / save this result". Never gate the answer behind an email.
- **Double opt-in** with a welcome email that sets expectations (frequency, what a deal looks like, how to whitelist). This protects deliverability and meets Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) express-consent and identification requirements, including a working unsubscribe link.

**Honest social proof (allowed under the spec):**
- Live "Recently posted deals" ticker drawn from real /deals/ entries (route plus date posted). It proves activity without inventing numbers.
- "Still working?" reader reports on deal posts (moderated).
- Transparency: "How we find deals", "Last verified" dates, and named sources (official airline and government links).
- Third-party mentions only once they exist. **No invented subscriber counts or testimonials.**

### (c) Design and UX patterns

- **Colour:** most deal brands use one saturated action colour (orange, coral or bright blue) on white or very light backgrounds, with dark navy text. Recommendation: a sky-blue or navy base with one warm accent (orange or coral) reserved for CTAs and "deal" badges. Keep red for warnings only (e.g. "basic fare: no carry-on").
- **Hero:** a single job, the airport selector plus email. A secondary link "Search flights now" goes to the launcher.
- **Deal cards:** destination photo or illustrated header, route in large type (YYZ → LIS), a deal-threshold badge ("Deal: under $650 return"), a typical range in small text, travel months, and posted date. Cards should be fully tappable on mobile, with at least 44 px tap targets.
- **Tables:** comparison tables (carry-on sizes, APPR bands, airline fares and bags) with sticky headers and horizontal scroll inside a container on phones. The page itself must never scroll sideways.
- **Callouts:** "Pro tip" or "Heads up" blockquotes (already in the spec) break up long guides, as the best points blogs do.
- **Freshness UI:** an "Updated Nov 1, 2026 · Verified against airline sites" line under titles on fact-heavy pages.
- **Mobile:** most deal traffic arrives from email and social on phones. Use single-column layout, the thumb-zone sticky CTA bar, fast loading (static Jekyll pages are an advantage over JavaScript-heavy competitors) and lazy-loaded images and ads.
- **Ads:** keep AdSense units out of the hero and away from signup forms, with fixed slot heights to avoid layout shift. Competitor sites that cram ads near CTAs feel spammy.

### (d) Content gaps CheapFly can win in Canada

Observed in 2026 search results:

1. **Accurate, dated carry-on rules.** Many results come from luggage retailers and programmatic sites, and several show **outdated numbers**: WestJet's pre-May-2025 size, TAP's old 8 kg limit, conflicting PAL weights. A "verified on" carry-on table with official links is a clear win. See `_data/carryon.yml`.
2. **"Basic fare = no carry-on" across Canadian airlines.** Air Canada (Jan 2025), WestJet UltraBasic, Porter Basic and Air Transat Eco Budget (Feb 2025) all exclude a full carry-on on North American and sun routes, and Flair charges for one. A single comparison of **what the cheapest fare really costs with a bag** is a high-value page and tool output.
3. **Neutral APPR guidance.** The search results are crowded with claims companies charging commissions and with pages that **blur proposed rules with current law**. CheapFly can own "how to claim yourself, free" plus a box on what is pending (the Gazette draft and Bill C-31) and what isn't in force.
4. **"Which Canadian airlines still fly in 2026."** Swoop, Lynx and Jetlines are gone, Sunwing is now part of WestJet, Flair is repositioning, Porter is growing at YYZ and in the west, and the US carrier Spirit shut down in May 2026. Searchers are confused, and few Canadian pages keep a single current list.
5. **Airport-level money pages.** Narcity and MTL Blog cover bag fees at YYZ and YUL, but there is little for YEG, YHZ, YWG, YQB, YXE, YQR and YYT. CheapFly's 16 airport pages can include airlines, bag rules, nearby alternatives and deal thresholds.
6. **US border-airport strategy after Spirit.** Older "drive to Buffalo or Bellingham" advice often relied on Spirit. It needs a refresh covering which airlines remain and the true cost after parking, exchange rates and baggage.
7. **French-language content.** Milesopedia and Flytrippers serve Québec bilingually. Most deal sites don't. Start with YUL and YQB airport pages, the APPR guide and the carry-on tool.
8. **EU261 for Canadians.** Canadians flying to Europe are often covered by EU261 on the way back (and on EU airlines). It is rarely explained from a Canadian angle.
