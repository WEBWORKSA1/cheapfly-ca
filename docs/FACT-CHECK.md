## 2. APPR summary (as in force on 2026-11-01)

**Bottom line: the compensation amounts have NOT changed.** The 2019 regulations (SOR/2019-150, last amended Sept 8, 2022) still apply. The Justice Laws consolidation is current to Sept 30, 2026. Machine-readable values are in `_data/appr.yml`.

| Situation (within airline control, not safety) | Large carrier | Small carrier |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive 3 to <6 h late | $400 | $125 |
| Arrive 6 to <9 h late | $700 | $250 |
| Arrive 9 h+ late | $1,000 | $500 |
| Refund taken instead of rebooking | $400 | $125 |

Denied boarding (within control), any carrier size: **$900** (<6 h), **$1,800** (6 to <9 h), **$2,400** (9 h+), payable within 48 hours.

- Large carrier: 2 million or more passengers worldwide in each of the two preceding calendar years.
- Claim window: 1 year. The airline must respond within 30 days. If it does not, or you disagree, complain to the CTA.
- No compensation is owed for disruptions that are **required for safety** or **outside the airline's control**. Rebooking, or a refund when rebooking within 48 h is impossible, is still owed in those cases.
- Meals, communication and a hotel are owed after a 2 h wait for disruptions within control (including safety-related ones).
- Large carriers must rebook on a competitor if they cannot rebook within 9 h.
- Tarmac delays: passengers must be let off after 3 h (plus up to 45 min if take-off is imminent).

**Pending changes, NOT in force (do not present as current law):**

- **Proposed APPR overhaul:** [Canada Gazette Part I, Dec 21, 2024](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2024/2024-12-21/html/reg1-eng.html), comments closed Mar 6, 2025. It would remove the three categories, make compensation the default unless "exceptional circumstances" apply, and raise maximum corporate penalties from $25,000 to $250,000. Reports through Oct 2026 say it still has not come into force. One CTA document refers to a "December 2025" Part I publication, but the Gazette URL is dated 2024, so use the 2024 date.
- **Bill C-31** (Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 2, Division 17): would transfer APPR rule-making and complaint resolution from the CTA to the Minister of Transport and minister-approved third parties. It passed second reading on June 3, 2026. The Senate transport committee studied it in September 2026 (report due Oct 9), and late-October coverage still described it as before Parliament. The CTA complaint backlog is about **97,000 to 98,000**.

**Writers:** always add "(verify before you fly)" and a general-information disclaimer. Do not promise outcomes or timelines for CTA complaints.

## 4. Trademark scan: CHEAPFLY / CHEAP FLY / CHEAPFLIGHTS

### What could and could not be done

The planned searches were the CIPO Canadian Trademarks Database, USPTO trademark search and a general web search. **None of them could run in this session**: direct page fetching was blocked and the search budget was used up. The assessment below is preliminary, based on Canadian trademark principles and general marketplace knowledge. It is **not legal advice and not a clearance opinion.**

### Manual searches to run before investing in the brand (about 30 minutes)

1. **CIPO:** <https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/srch>. Search `CHEAPFLY`, `CHEAP FLY`, `CHEAPFLIGHT*`, `CHEAP FLIGHT*` and `*FLY.CA`, using the "contains" search type, in Nice classes **39** (travel arrangement, booking) and **35** (advertising, comparison services), plus 9, 41 and 42. Record each owner, its status (registered, pending or abandoned) and the goods and services covered.
2. **USPTO:** <https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/>. Same terms, live and dead records.
3. **EUIPO/WIPO Global Brand Database:** <https://branddb.wipo.int/>, for international registrations that may extend to Canada (Canada joined the Madrid Protocol in 2019).
4. **Web and domain check:** search for "cheapfly" and "cheap fly" businesses, social handles and app-store listings in Canada. Check for active sites on `cheapfly.com` and similar domains.
5. If any live CHEAPFLIGHTS or CHEAPFLY mark covers travel or flight-comparison services in Canada, get a one-hour opinion from a Canadian trademark agent (a registered "trademark agent", listed by CIPO).

### Preliminary risk assessment

- **Main conflict risk: CHEAPFLIGHTS.** Background, not re-verified: "Cheapflights" is a long-established flight-comparison brand in the KAYAK / Booking Holdings group and has operated country sites, historically including a Canadian one. It very likely holds trademark rights in several countries. "CheapFly" shares the same leading element and sounds similar, and it would offer **the same kind of service (flight search and deals) to the same customers**. Under the Canadian confusion test (*Trademarks Act* s.6(5): resemblance, nature of services and trade, distinctiveness, length of use), the overlap in services and trade is the factor that raises risk.
- **What lowers the risk:** both marks are built from descriptive words ("cheap" + "fly/flights"). Canadian courts and examiners give descriptive marks narrow protection, and small differences can be enough to tell them apart. CheapFly.ca is a content and newsletter site, not a booking engine. A distinct visual identity, colours and logo reduce confusion.
- **Registering CHEAPFLY ourselves would be hard:** it is likely to be refused as **clearly descriptive** (s.12(1)(b)) of "cheap flights" services unless it gains distinctiveness through use. A **stylized logo mark** has a better chance than the plain word.
- **Verdict: MODERATE risk.** It is low enough to keep operating under CheapFly.ca, but not low enough to skip a formal search, especially before paid marketing, an app or merchandise. The highest-risk behaviours are visual resemblance to the Cheapflights brand, using "Cheapflights" in metadata, ads or keywords, and buying search ads on competitors' brand terms. All of these are avoidable.

### Recommended branding rules

- Always write the brand as **"CheapFly.ca"** or **"CheapFly"** (one word, capital F). Never "Cheap Flights", "CheapFlights" or "Cheapflights Canada".
- Use a distinct logo and colour palette, and avoid palettes and wordmark styles used by the large metasearch brands.
- Never bid on, target or mention competitor brand names in ads, page titles or meta tags (this matches the content spec's ban on the word "Cheapflights").
- Tagline idea that stresses independence: "Independent cheap-flight alerts for Canadians."

### Recommended disclaimer wording (site footer and About page)

> CheapFly.ca is an independent Canadian travel information website. We are not a travel agency and do not sell tickets. CheapFly.ca is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by any airline, airport, online travel agency or flight search engine. Airline, airport and company names are used for identification only, and all trademarks belong to their respective owners. Fares and policies change often: always confirm the price, baggage rules and conditions with the airline or booking site before you buy.

Shorter version for deal posts and emails:

> Independent site, not affiliated with any airline or booking company. Prices are examples seen at the time of writing and can change or disappear without notice.

Add an affiliate disclosure near any affiliate link once affiliate programs are joined, for example: "Some links may earn CheapFly.ca a commission at no extra cost to you." This follows Competition Bureau guidance on clear, close-to-the-link disclosure.
